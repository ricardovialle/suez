Report · smooth conditions

Age-varying eQTLs in one mixed model

Fit the gene's expression trajectory and the genetic effect as smooth functions of age, test whether the eQTL changes with age, and read back both curves with standard errors. Below: how to run it, and what it gains over the previous default, where age entered as a single straight line.

Yi = μ(agei) + β(agei) × Gi + ZiTγ + uind + εi
Previous default · numeric condition μ(age) = a·age   β(age) = β₀ + β₁·age

One G×age term, 1 df. Detects effects that grow or shrink steadily with age.

New · spline condition μ(age) = Σ ajBj(age)   β(age) = β₀ + Σ βjBj(age)

Natural-spline basis B (here 3 columns), joint G×basis test with 3 df. Detects onsets, plateaus and mid-life peaks.

The other half of the model

Expression trajectory μ(age)

True μ(age)LinearSplineSpline 95% CI

The same fit returns the gene's age trajectory, adjusted for genotype, kinship, repeated samples and latent confounders. The linear model can only draw a straight line through a rise and fall; the spline follows it.

The intercept is not estimated, so μ is relative: suez reports it against the youngest age in the data. Curves here are centred on their mean for comparison.

A wrong μ(age) is also a likely reason for the linear model's mild inflation in the null panel above: trajectory left unexplained ends up in the variance components the interaction test relies on.

When the spline helps

  • Effects with an onset or a plateau, such as an eQTL that switches on after 60: about 3 times as many hits at a genome-wide threshold.
  • Effects that peak and return in mid-life: the linear test is close to its false-positive rate, the spline finds over half.
  • Non-linear expression trajectories, which are common with age. A misfit μ(age) can inflate a linear test.
  • Interpretation: you get β at every age with a confidence band, not just a slope.

What it costs

  • Degrees of freedom. 3 df instead of 1. Here this cost no power for truly linear effects, but with fewer samples it can.
  • Noisier curves at the edges when the truth is a line: slightly higher curve error for linear and constant shapes.
  • Sparse ages. Splines need samples across the range. Choose df (2 to 4) and knots by the age distribution, not by fit.
  • Run time is about the same: for this simulation.
  • Neighbouring SNPs. In genes with a strong age-varying eQTL, null SNPs nearby were inflated under both models (λ 0.95 to 1.37), because the unmodelled effect makes residual variance depend on age.
